# Finite sums

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}k=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\label{eq:sum}$$

> [!PROPOSITION] {#prop:sum} Adding nonnegative values
>
> A sum of nonnegative values remains nonnegative. See $\autoref{thm:energy}$
> and $\autoref{eq:sum}$.

> [!EXAMPLE] {#ex:sum} Two terms
>
> The values $1$ and $2$ give a sum of $3$.
>
> | Term | Value |
> | --- | --- |
> | First | 1 |
> | Second | 2 |
