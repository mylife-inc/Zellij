# Return to the page

## A passage

> A mark in the margin is a conversation with a future reader. Sometimes that
> reader is someone else; often it is ourselves, returning with different questions.

## Commentary

[Reflection](glossary:reflection) does not require an immediate conclusion.
A useful note can be a question, a connection or a point of disagreement.
As $\autoref{remark:reading}$ suggests, interpretation remains open to revision.

> [!EXAMPLE] {#example:journal} A three-line notebook
>
> Record one phrase that stayed with you, one question it raised and one
> small action you could try. Return a week later and add a fourth line.

## A practice for the week

| Moment | Invitation |
| --- | --- |
| Before reading | Name the question you bring. |
| During reading | Mark a detail that surprises you. |
| After reading | Write one sentence in your own words. |

A [reflection note](index:reflection) can connect today's reading with a passage
from an earlier chapter. Leave enough space for a later response.
